Published in [*Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11205-z) on 21 October 2026.

Pinal uses natural-language instructions to generate structures and then design protein sequences. Its 16B parameters were trained on 1.7 billion synthetically annotated protein-text pairs. Experimental tests confirmed the requested functions for a fluorescent protein, a PET hydrolase, an alcohol dehydrogenase, and a metabolic H-protein.

In a multi-enzyme CO2 fixation pathway, a designed H-protein delivered 1.7 times the product titre obtained with the corresponding native *E. coli* H-protein, without iterative experimental optimization.

\* Fengyuan Dai and Shiyang You contributed equally.
